Blackbox logging records gyro, PID, motor output, and RC input at high
sample rates directly to onboard flash or an SD card. It turns "the quad
feels weird" into something you can actually diagnose.

## Before you open the log

Fly a short, deliberate test flight: a few punch-outs, some fast direction
changes, and a bit of hover. A log full of gentle cruising doesn't stress the
tune enough to show problems.

## What to look at first

Start with the gyro trace against setpoint on one axis (usually roll).
A well-tuned axis tracks setpoint closely with minimal overshoot and no
persistent oscillation after a sharp stick input. Fuzzy thickening on the
gyro trace points to filtering, not PID — see *Understanding Gyro & D-term
Filters* in the Knowledge Hub before touching P/I/D values.

## Motor output traces

Check that motor outputs aren't clipping at 0% or 100% during normal flying
— clipping means the tune or throttle range is asking for more correction
than the motors can deliver, and no amount of PID tuning fixes that; it's a
power or prop-loading problem.

## Step response

If your logging tool supports a PID step-response plot, it's the fastest way
to see overshoot and settling time per axis at a glance, without having to
eyeball raw traces across the whole flight.
