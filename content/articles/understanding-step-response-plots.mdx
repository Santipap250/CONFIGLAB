A step-response plot answers one specific question per axis: when the
stick makes a sudden, sharp move, how does the actual rotation rate chase
the commanded one? It's a more direct read on tuning quality than eyeballing
raw gyro traces.

## Reading the curve

A well-tuned axis rises quickly to the commanded rate, settles with little
or no overshoot, and holds flat. Excess overshoot (the curve spiking past
the target before settling back) generally points to too much P relative to
D; slow, sluggish rise toward the target points to too little P.

## Ringing after the step

A curve that oscillates repeatedly around the target before settling —
"ringing" — is one of the clearest blackbox signatures of D-term set too
high for the current filter quality, and is often mistaken for a P problem
because it also looks like general oscillation.

## Comparing across axes

Roll and pitch step responses often differ meaningfully on the same
airframe, since they see different arm lengths, mass distribution, and prop
wash exposure. Judge each axis's step response independently rather than
assuming one setting should be identical across all three.

## When it doesn't line up with "flight feel"

A close-to-ideal step response and a quad that still feels off in the air
usually point away from PID and toward filtering, mechanical issues, or
rates shaping — see the Tuning Guide for the intended order of operations.
