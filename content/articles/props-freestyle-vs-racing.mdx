Prop choice changes thrust curve, efficiency, and noise signature — all of
which feed back into how the airframe should be tuned.

## Pitch

Higher pitch moves more air per rotation at a given RPM, giving higher top
speed but drawing more current and heating motors faster under load. Racing
builds often lean into higher pitch for straight-line speed; freestyle builds
frequently favor a lower pitch for smoother, more predictable power delivery
through tricks.

## Blade count

Tri-blade and quad-blade props generally give smoother, more predictable
thrust and better low-speed authority than bi-blades, at the cost of some
efficiency and top speed. Freestyle pilots often prefer tri-blades for the
extra control feel in slow, technical maneuvers.

## Material

Stiffer props (e.g. polycarbonate blends) transmit less flex-related noise
into the gyro signal, which can mean a cleaner blackbox trace and less
reliance on aggressive filtering — but they're also less forgiving on
impact than more flexible props.

## The tuning connection

Changing prop pitch or blade count shifts the motor's noise frequency and
the airframe's response characteristics — treat any prop swap as a reason to
revisit dynamic notch range and re-check the tune, not just a cosmetic
change.
