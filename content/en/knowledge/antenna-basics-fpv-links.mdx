FPV setups run two separate RF links — video (analog or digital, typically
5.8GHz) and control (typically 2.4GHz, e.g. ELRS/Crossfire) — and both
depend heavily on antenna choice, not just transmitter power.

## Polarization

Circular polarization (RHCP/LHCP) resists multipath interference from
reflections much better than linear polarization, which is why nearly all
modern FPV video antennas are circularly polarized. Matching polarization
between transmitter and receiver antenna matters — mismatched polarization
causes a real signal loss even at short range.

## Gain and pattern

Higher-gain antennas focus RF energy into a narrower cone, extending range
directly ahead at the cost of coverage to the sides and behind — a poor
trade for freestyle flying close to obstacles, but often a good one for
long, straight-line range flights.

## Orientation

Whip and cloverleaf-style antennas should generally be mounted vertically
and kept clear of carbon fiber (which blocks RF) and other antennas, since
antenna-to-antenna interference and self-blocking by the frame are common,
easily fixed causes of a link that seems to fail well inside its rated
range.
