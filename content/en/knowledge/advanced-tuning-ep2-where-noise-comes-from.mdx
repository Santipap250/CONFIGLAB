Once you know noise is the silent enemy of your drone, the next question
is: where does it actually come from? Why do some builds have far more
noise than others that look nearly identical?

The answer lives in these spots.

## 1) ESCs + motors (the #1 source of noise)

ESCs switch at very high frequencies, and every time you add or reduce
throttle, interference is generated immediately. A low-quality ESC, no
capacitor, or long/poorly-twisted wiring makes noise spike right away. See
<a href="/knowledge/motor-esc-basics">Motor &amp; ESC Basics</a> for the
underlying protocol and RPM-filtering fundamentals that help here.

## 2) Wire routing (a small thing that can wreck the whole build)

High-current power wires routed close to the gyro, camera cable, or VTX
cable feed noise straight into the flight controller without you ever
noticing. A build with clean wire routing is almost always noticeably
calmer.

## 3) Mounting the FC too rigidly

Bolting the FC down hard with no rubber grommets or soft-mount lets frame
vibration transmit straight into the gyro. The result: the gyro reads
garbage, PID is constantly over-correcting, and the quad shakes in a way
that's hard to trace back to a cause.

## 4) Props and frame

Unbalanced props, or a frame that's slightly warped or cracked, create
mechanical noise that's genuinely hard for filters to clean up. See
<a href="/knowledge/gyro-dterm-filters">Understanding Gyro &amp; D-term
Filters</a> for why mechanical noise specifically is a limit on what
filtering alone can fix.

## 5) VTX and camera

A high-power VTX without proper power isolation lets noise flow back into
the rest of the system. Symptoms include video tearing, glitching frames,
and gyro readings drifting without any obvious cause.

## The truth a lot of people don't want to accept

> Most noise doesn't come from a Betaflight setting — it comes from how
> the build was actually put together.

## EP2 summary

Noise is both electrical and mechanical. ESCs and wiring are the primary
sources. Build quality matters just as much as PID values do.
