A flight controller doesn't fly your quad — it constantly compares where the
gyro says the craft *is* against where your stick input says it *should be*,
and corrects the error. The PID loop is how that correction is shaped.

## P — Proportional

Proportional term reacts to the **size of the current error**. Higher P means
a sharper, more immediate correction to any deviation from the setpoint. Too
much P and the quad feels twitchy or oscillates at a fast, tight frequency;
too little and it feels sloppy or floaty under wind and prop wash.

## I — Integral

Integral term reacts to **accumulated error over time** — it's what holds an
angle against a constant disturbance, like wind or a slightly bent frame arm.
Too much I causes a slow, wallowing oscillation (often called "I-term
bounce"); too little and the quad drifts or sags out of a hold, especially
noticeable in punch-outs and stalls.

## D — Derivative

Derivative term reacts to the **rate of change of the error**, effectively
damping the P term so the craft doesn't overshoot its target. Too much D
amplifies high-frequency noise (motor heat, prop hop); too little lets P
overshoot and oscillate.

## Why order matters

Tune in this order: get filters clean first (see *Gyro & D-term Filters*),
then P, then D, then I. Chasing P/D against a noisy, unfiltered gyro signal
means you're tuning against noise, not against the airframe.
