description: "Why a tune that feels great at half throttle can turn twitchy at full send — and how TPA fixes it."

PID gains that feel perfectly damped at moderate throttle often start
oscillating at full throttle, because motor thrust and prop loading don't
scale linearly with stick input. TPA reduces PID gains as throttle rises
past a set point, to compensate.

## The two settings

`tpa_rate` sets how much the PID gains are reduced at full throttle,
expressed as a percentage. `tpa_breakpoint` sets the throttle value (in raw
PWM units, typically 1000–2000) where attenuation starts kicking in — below
that point, PID gains are unaffected.

## Reading the symptom

If a quad is clean and stable through most of the throttle range but starts
oscillating specifically on hard punch-outs or at wide-open throttle, that's
the classic TPA symptom — not a P or D problem at the gains you're already
running.

## Tuning approach

Get the base tune dialed in around mid-throttle first. Only then introduce
TPA, raising `tpa_rate` gradually while testing full-throttle punch-outs,
rather than trying to solve the same problem by lowering P or D globally
and losing sharpness everywhere else.
